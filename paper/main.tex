\begin{abstract}
Wuebben (arXiv:2608.17267), building on Lidman--Piccirillo
(arXiv:2505.14387), proves that a specified Lidman--Piccirillo piece $V$
--- a symplectic 4-manifold with the homology of $S^2\times D^2$, built
from a non-product genus-2 surface bundle over a once-punctured torus by
two Luttinger surgeries --- is simply connected; the symplectic double of
$V$ is then homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to $S^2\times S^2$, and
Lidman--Piccirillo regluing yields an exotic
$\CP^2\#\overline{\CP}{}^2$. The critical step, historically the failure
point of claimed small exotic 4-manifolds, is the simple connectivity:
the triviality of the filled fundamental groups for the paper's permitted
surgery parametrizations. We report a verification of that step
independent of the paper's coordinates, software, and author: the bundle
is rebuilt as a simplicial complex from the paper's marked-fiber data
alone; the surgery tori, based meridians, and framed longitudes are
extracted combinatorially; and the four theorem-critical filled
presentations are proved trivial by complete confluent rewriting, with
every computation emitting a replayable proof certificate validated by
two independently written checkers. Four adjacent half-drift
presentations pass the same certification as an auxiliary check. The
author's committed scripts enter at exactly one point, parsed and never
executed, as a cross-check of the dictionary of conventions read from the
paper. The paper's framing lemma is machine-checked with its analytic
normalizations rebuilt constructively and chart independence proved
between the paper's displayed charts, and a finite 100-case robustness
scan quantifies the consequences of any framing discrepancy. Stated
carefully: the simple-connectivity proof appears complete relative to
explicitly named standard topology theorems, with no known
project-specific gap. The theorem is Wuebben's; this note claims only the
verification. All certificates, code, and provenance are public in a
pinned release.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Claimed constructions of small exotic 4-manifolds have repeatedly failed
in one place: the fundamental-group computation. The manifolds of
\cite{Wuebben} rest on a specified Lidman--Piccirillo piece $V$, obtained
by two Luttinger surgeries \cite{Luttinger,ADK} on Lagrangian tori in a
symplectic non-product genus-2 surface bundle $R$ over a once-punctured
torus, following the framework of \cite{LidmanPiccirillo}. Exoticness of
the double of $V$ rests on $V$ being simply connected: the filled
fundamental groups of the paper's permitted surgery parametrizations ---
the four sign choices of the double surgery in the paper's principal
coordinate system --- must be trivial. Four adjacent ``half-drift''
presentations of the same fillings serve as re-indexing checks; they
carry no independent logical load.

This note reports an independent verification of that step, together
with a machine audit of the framing lemma that connects the group theory
to the symplectic geometry. Independence here is concrete: nothing in
the verification reuses the paper's coordinates, code, or intermediate
data. The geometry was rebuilt from the paper's stated marked-fiber data
alone, and the paper's displayed relations, sign tables, and Table~1
filling words were then \emph{compared against} the rebuild rather than
assumed. The downstream deductions --- from simple connectivity to the
homeomorphism classifications and the exoticness conclusions --- are
Wuebben's; this note verifies the simple-connectivity input and its
geometric framing bridge, and a hypothesis audit of the downstream
deductions is recorded in the repository.

Two disclosures frame everything that follows. First, the theorem is
Wuebben's; this note asserts nothing about priority or authorship of the
mathematics and claims only the verification. Second, the verification
was carried out by generative-AI systems under the author's direction,
with commit-level provenance recorded in the repository's ledger;
mathematical responsibility rests with the human author. No claim below
depends on trusting a model: every asserted computation ships with a
replayable certificate.

\begin{mainresult}
From a fresh checkout of release \texttt{v1.1} of the verification
repository (Section~\ref{sec:trust}):
(i) the four theorem-critical filled presentations --- the $n{=}0$ sign
pairs of the paper's double surgery, imposed with independently certified
based meridians and framed longitudes --- carry complete confluent
rewriting certificates proving each group trivial, and the certificates
replay and are accepted by two independently written checkers;
(ii) the four adjacent $n{=}1$ half-drift presentations carry the same
certificates, as an auxiliary check;
(iii) the external inputs are the named standard theorems of
Section~\ref{sec:trust}, which this note does not reprove;
(iv) every certificate, transcript, and the provenance ledger are
contained in that pinned release.
\end{mainresult}

\section{The independent model and the interpretation
dictionary}\label{sec:model}

The genus-2 fiber is realized as a regular neighborhood of the paper's
five-chain of curves: five plumbed annular bands and two cone discs, with
the hyperelliptic-type involution $\varphi_0$ realized simplicially. Its
fixed points, the curve actions $a\leftrightarrow e$,
$b\leftrightarrow d$, and the free half-rotation of the middle curve $c$
are executable assertions about the complex, not assumptions. The bundle
$R$ is assembled from simplicial mapping cylinders over the once-punctured
torus base; the two surgery tori $T_\alpha$, $T_\beta$ arise as induced
subcomplexes, and the assembled 4-complex passes manifold checks down to
the vertex-link level.

The identification with the paper's marked bundle is by literal based
monodromy action: the based mapping classes of both monodromies are
certified as equalities of vertex paths, transported by proof-producing
Tietze chains that replay step by step. The identification is completed
by an explicit marked mapping-cylinder clutching over the base graph: the
relative monodromy comparisons are exact on the marked collars, so the
identification is a constructed fiberwise homeomorphism into the paper's
bundle rather than an appeal to the classification of surface bundles.
Everything the surgery argument needs is transported through that map
into the paper's already-smooth manifold, where the smooth and symplectic
arguments run; no smoothing of the triangulation is ever chosen.

Because the machine certifies properties of a model, the model itself is
the residual risk. Five structurally different implementations
reconstruct it independently --- among them an independent peripheral
extractor built only from the bundle, an alternative triangulation with
its own based-monodromy certificate, and a second marked-fiber
realization built directly from the abstract ribbon graph --- and they
are structured so that the classes of translation error they can detect
--- subdivision artifacts, monodromy transcription, ordering, sign, and
orientation conventions --- would each be caught by at least one
independent reconstruction. What redundancy cannot exclude, a shared
misreading of the paper's conventions, is addressed by a bound
interpretation dictionary: every convention read from the paper carries
the certified computation that would fail under a rival reading, with the
central entries cross-checked --- parsed, never executed --- against the
author's own committed scripts, and the remaining transcription-level
entries audited by a quarantined extractor against the hash-pinned
immutable Lidman--Piccirillo v1 arXiv source \cite{LidmanPiccirillo}, in
both its prose and its vector-figure layers. The one interpretation
remaining at that boundary is the ordinary convention that dashed arcs in
a handle picture depict hidden projection rather than extra crossings.
The full inventory of the redundant implementations and the dictionary is
in the repository.

\section{Peripheral data and the framing
identification}\label{sec:peripheral}

Based meridians and product-framing longitudes for both tori are traced
as literal simplicial loops carrying the paper's own basing whiskers. The
paper's sign-correction package (its \S7.4 fix) is reproduced
independently, and a combinatorial sweep of the relevant transport square
resolves the basing double-count question in the direction the paper's
corrected convention requires. Both surgery tori are certified locally
flat at every one of their simplex links, each classified against the
correct unknotted model, and every extracted dual meridian is checked to
be a literal normal-circle fiber of an explicit normal block model, with
no regular-neighborhood theorem invoked.

Luttinger surgery is performed with respect to the Lagrangian framing;
the paper's Lemma~8.2 identifies it with the fibered framing carried by
the combinatorial longitudes above. This lemma carries an exact logical
load: if the identification failed by $j$ meridians, the certificates of
Section~\ref{sec:groups} would remain correct for the product-framed
presentations they literally describe, but would no longer identify
those presentations with the paper's surgeries. The lemma's displayed
Weinstein-chart, seam, and constant-momentum algebra are machine-checked
symbolically, and its inline analytic normalizations have been rebuilt
constructively rather than cited: the relative Moser flow is produced as
the explicit monotone inverse of a cumulative coordinate, so no ODE
existence theorem is invoked, and the equivariant step defines the Moser
field directly on the double cover with certified projection and deck
invariance, so no covering-lift theorem is invoked. Chart independence
is proved between the paper's displayed charts by two routes, with the
one symplectic move that could introduce meridian components --- the
closed-1-form shift --- excluded by the zero-section condition;
\cite{ADK} (\S2.1 and Proposition~2.2) stands as corroboration.

Independently of the lemma, a finite 100-case robustness scan refills the
complement with meridian-shifted longitudes and runs the identical
certification pipeline on each counterfactual: 82 of the 100 cases are
certified trivial, none is nontrivial, every shift touching only
$T_\beta$ is certified trivial in both families, and each of the 18
undecided cases is perfect, admits no epimorphism onto the six smallest
nonabelian simple groups, and resists coset enumeration to a ceiling of
$12{,}499{,}998$ cosets. These are counterfactual groups: the paper's own
fillings are the certified controls, and the undecided cases have no
bearing on the proof unless the framing argument above first fails. The
full table and methods are in the repository.

\section{Certificate verification and
reproducibility}\label{sec:certificates}

Knuth--Bendix completion (KBMAG \cite{KBMAG}, run under GAP \cite{GAP})
is used only as an \emph{untrusted certificate generator}: the exported
derivation DAGs are validated by a small independent checker that
re-verifies every input-relator axiom, inverse axiom, critical overlap,
rewrite trace, and final generator-to-identity rule, and binds the input
presentation by SHA-256. The checker neither imports nor runs KBMAG. A
second implementation, written independently in Ruby, re-verifies all
retained derivation records against the same hash-bound DAGs and accepts,
importing neither the first checker nor any project group code. All
artifacts --- the certificates, the exported rewriting systems, and a
hash manifest --- replay from a clean checkout, and the stored rewriting
systems reload and re-verify under GAP/KBMAG independently of the
certificate path.

The verification was AI-assisted throughout, by generative-AI systems
working under the author's direction; the repository's provenance ledger
records which system produced which artifact, and preserves the
corrections that adversarial cross-model review produced rather than
rewriting them. Mathematical responsibility rests with the human author.
The design principle throughout is that certificates are checked, not
trusted: search and completion tools are treated as untrusted generators,
and every computational claim rests on a replayable artifact validated by
small independent code.

\section{Trust boundary and data availability}\label{sec:trust}

The named standard inputs, applied with checked hypotheses: Freedman's
simply connected classification \cite{Freedman} with the
Hambleton--Kreck finite-cyclic classification \cite{HK88,HK93};
symplectic Kodaira dimension and minimality, via the audited
Luttinger-surgery constructions \cite{ADK,HoLi}; the symplectic Thom
theorem \cite{OzsvathSzabo}; the Rokhlin/Arf input of the slicing
argument, as used in \cite{LidmanPiccirillo}; the Ozsv\'ath--Szab\'o /
Lidman--Piccirillo Floer machinery \cite{LidmanPiccirillo}; and the
Ker\'ekj\'art\'o periodic-disk involution theorem \cite{ConstantinKolev}
behind the disk extension of the paper's Lemma~7.1. Alongside these sit
elementary inputs of PL topology retained as standard without a specific
citation: the ribbon-thickening and bistellar-trace interpretations that
read the paper's figures into complexes, the classification of surfaces,
the collapsible-3-ball and cyclic-knot-unknot criteria behind the
local-flatness certificates, and simplicial fundamental-group
presentation with Tietze theory. This note reproves none of these. The
ribbon-interpretation input is narrower than the phrase suggests: the
certified classification of equivariant ribbon rotations pins the
implication from the paper's stated marked five-chain data to the
equivariant normal form, and the stated data themselves are audited in
the original Lidman--Piccirillo source, so what this boundary retains is
the thickening of that data into complexes together with the ordinary
dashed-arc hidden-projection convention of the audited figure. The
accurate one-sentence status is: \emph{the simple-connectivity proof
appears complete relative to explicitly named standard topology
theorems, with no known project-specific gap} --- a statement about
verification depth, offered to facilitate expert review, not to replace
it.

All code, certificates, run transcripts, referee packets, and the
provenance ledger are contained, with clean-checkout replay
instructions, in the tagged release \texttt{v1.1} of
\url{https://github.com/VentiMath/exotic-s2xs2-verification}; the
certificate manifest binds every artifact by SHA-256. The paper's own code is at
\url{https://github.com/bwuebben/exotic-s2xs2}; nothing in the
verification derives from it except where explicitly cited.
