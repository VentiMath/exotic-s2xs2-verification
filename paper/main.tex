\begin{abstract}
We define a compact marked surface-bundle surgery manifold
$V_{\mathrm{aud}}$, prove that $\pione(V_{\mathrm{aud}})=1$, and double it
along an explicit involution $\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$ of its boundary.  The
closed manifold
$Z_{\mathrm{aud}}=V_{\mathrm{aud}}\cup_{\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}}V_{\mathrm{aud}}$
is simply connected and spin with intersection form $H$, hence homeomorphic
to $S^2\times S^2$ by Freedman; it is a closed genus-two surface bundle over
a genus-two surface surgered along four Lagrangian tori, hence symplectic
with symplectic Kodaira dimension at least zero by
Auroux--Donaldson--Katzarkov, Liu, and Ho--Li; hence, since Li's Kodaira
dimension is an invariant of the oriented diffeomorphism type, it is not
diffeomorphic to $S^2\times S^2$.  The final classification and
non-diffeomorphism deductions use named external theorems whose hypotheses
are discharged in the text.  Simple connectivity
itself rests on four explicit finite presentations, trivial under a
published derivation-certificate specification, one of which a geometric
reconstruction identifies with $\pione(V_{\mathrm{aud}})$.  Automated
certificate replay assumes that at least one checker conforms to the
mathematical specification; the two shipped implementations have not
received an independent human line-by-line audit.  The construction was
motivated by Wuebben's proposed exotic $S^2\times S^2$; whether
$Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is his manifold is an attribution question, recorded
clause by clause as checklist D1--D14 in the supplement, on which no
theorem here depends.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

This paper constructs a closed symplectic four-manifold homeomorphic but
not diffeomorphic to $S^2\times S^2$.  The construction has three layers.
A compact marked surface-bundle surgery manifold $V_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is
defined from explicit data (Definition~\ref{def:Vaudit}) and proved simply
connected: finite derivation certificates first prove that four explicit
presentations are trivial, and a geometric argument then reconstructs the
marked bundle, torus exterior, peripheral curves, and drilled transport
relation, and identifies one fixed presentation with
$\pione(V_{\mathrm{aud}})$.  Elementary topology and a separate framing
theorem give the intrinsic homology, spin, fiber, section, and symplectic
statements of Theorem~\ref{thm:audit-manifold}.  Finally
$V_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is doubled along an involution $\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$
of its boundary defined from the same data, and the double
$Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is shown to be homeomorphic to $S^2\times S^2$ and
symplectic of nonnegative Kodaira dimension (Theorem~\ref{thm:exotic}).
The other three presentations are algebraic robustness checks, not
additional geometric manifolds.

The construction was motivated by the disputed fundamental-group step in
Wuebben's proposed exotic-manifold construction \cite{Wuebben}, which
builds on the surgery scheme of Lidman and Piccirillo
\cite{LidmanPiccirillo}.  A contrary computation attributed to Lidman and
Piccirillo has been reported, but no presentation or nontriviality witness
for it is publicly available in the materials examined here.  This paper
does not adjudicate that dispute.  Its theorems concern the separately
defined $V_{\mathrm{aud}}$ and $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$; whether these coincide
with Wuebben's $V$ and his double is an attribution question, recorded as
the open checklist D1--D14 in the supplement, and no theorem here has a
clause of that checklist among its hypotheses.

Triviality is deliberately not used as evidence that a proposed
source-to-presentation dictionary is correct.  Wuebben's public
computations report trivial groups for all $72$ cases in one nearby grid and
classify $14$ of $15$ single-word perturbations as \textsc{silent} at the
group-decision layer \cite{WuebbenRepo}; the separate $100$-case
meridian-shift scan in this project likewise reports only trivial groups.
These experiments show that triviality is non-discriminating in this
neighborhood.  They do not support the source comparison.  Instead, the
identification of the fixed presentation with $V_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is proved
by the geometric reconstruction in
Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:peripheral}; the transfer to Wuebben's
member is the attribution question of Section~\ref{sec:relation}.

The logical architecture is summarized below.  Solid arrows are proved in
this article; the dashed arrow is the attribution question, which no
theorem here uses.

\section{The audit manifold and principal statements}\label{sec:audit-object}

Write $\Sigma_{1,1}$ for an oriented once-punctured torus with based
oriented spine loops $\alpha,\beta$.

\begin{definition}[the audit manifold]\label{def:Vaudit}
Let $F$ be a closed oriented genus-two surface with an ordered five-chain
$(a,b,c,d,e)$ whose regular-neighborhood complement is the disjoint union of
two disks containing marked points $p,O$.  Let $\varphi_0$ be the
orientation-preserving involution reversing the chain, fixing $p,O$, and
acting freely on $c$ by a half-rotation.  Let
\[
 \psi_0=T_a\circ T_b,
\]
with $T_b$ applied first and both twists supported away from a collar of $e$.
Form the marked bundle $R_{\mathrm{aud}}$ over $\Sigma_{1,1}$ by clutching
the $\alpha$- and $\beta$-mapping cylinders of $\varphi_0$ and $\psi_0$ to
one common marked fiber.

Inside $R_{\mathrm{aud}}$ put
\[
 T_\alpha=c\rtimes_{\varphi_0}S^1_\alpha,
 \qquad T_\beta=e\times S^1_\beta.
\]
Fix the basepoint $q=(p,*)$.  Let $A=[\bar\alpha]^{-1}$ and
$B=[\bar\beta]^{-1}$ be the based inverse base loops.  Base the oriented
meridians $M,N$ along the initial fiber arcs $y_1$ to $c_y$ and $s_2$ to
$s_e$, respectively.  In the product normal boundaries let
$\lambda_\alpha$ be the $y_1$-whiskered push-off that lifts the inverse
$\alpha$ direction and closes along the half of $c$ running from
$c_s=\varphi_0(c_y)$ to $c_y$, oriented from $c_s$ to $c_y$, and let
$\lambda_\beta$ be the $s_2$-whiskered push-off of the inverse $\beta$
direction along $e$.  Their based words $Ax$ and $(r^{-1}Mr)B$ are
conclusions of Lemmas~\ref{lem:alpha-peripheral} and
\ref{lem:beta-peripheral}, not part of this definition.  Define
$V_{\mathrm{aud}}$ by the product-framed fillings
\[
 M\lambda_\alpha=1,\qquad N\lambda_\beta=1.
\]
The orientations of $M,N,\lambda_\alpha,\lambda_\beta$ are part of this
definition.  Thus this is the $(\epsilon_A,\epsilon_B)=(+1,+1)$ sheet in the
fixed audit basis; changing one exponent while holding that basis fixed is
not declared to preserve the filling.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Audit-Manifold Theorem]\label{thm:audit-manifold}
The compact oriented smooth four-manifold $V_{\mathrm{aud}}$ has connected
boundary, Euler characteristic $2$, and
\[
 \pione(V_{\mathrm{aud}})=1,\qquad
 H_k(V_{\mathrm{aud}};\Z)\cong
 \begin{cases}
   \Z,&k=0,2,\\
   0,&k=1,3,4.
 \end{cases}
\]
It is spin.  A genus-two fiber $F$ disjoint from the surgery tori survives
as a primitive square-zero class generating $H_2(V_{\mathrm{aud}};\Z)$,
and the fixed point $p$ gives a properly embedded once-punctured-torus
section $\Gamma$ with $F\cdot\Gamma=1$.  Moreover
$V_{\mathrm{aud}}$ admits a symplectic form for which $F$ is symplectic.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:audit-manifold} is source-independent: it concerns the
explicit object in Definition~\ref{def:Vaudit}.  Simple connectivity and
the topological assertions are proved at the end of
Section~\ref{sec:peripheral}; symplecticity is proved after the logically
separate framing theorem in Section~\ref{sec:framing}.

\begin{theorem}[an exotic $S^2\times S^2$]\label{thm:exotic}
Let $\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$ be the involution $[z,t]\mapsto[\varphi_0(z),1-t]$
of the boundary mapping torus $\partial V_{\mathrm{aud}}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:sigma-aud}) and
$Z_{\mathrm{aud}}=V_{\mathrm{aud}}\cup_{\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}}V_{\mathrm{aud}}$.
Then $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is a closed symplectic four-manifold that is
homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to $S^2\times S^2$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:exotic} is proved in Section~\ref{sec:double} from
Theorem~\ref{thm:audit-manifold}, the framing theorem, and published
results whose hypotheses are discharged there one by one.  It is a
statement about the object defined here; its relation to Wuebben's proposed
manifold is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:relation} and enters no proof.

\section{The finite certificate theorem}\label{sec:certificates}

\begin{theorem}[finite-certificate statement]\label{thm:main}
Let $\mathcal M$ be the manifest identified in the normative-root paragraph
below.  Its sealed-presentation entry binds a three-generator,
seventy-eight-relator presentation $Q$ and four ordered pairs of filling
words indexed by
$(\epsilon_A,\epsilon_B)\in\{(+,+),(+,-),(-,+),(-,-)\}$.
Let $P_{\epsilon_A,\epsilon_B}$ be the presentation obtained from $Q$ by
adjoining the corresponding pair.  Every one of these four explicitly
manifest-bound presentations presents the trivial group.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[letters, words, and equation keys]\label{def:keys}
Fix generators $g_1,\ldots,g_n$.  Source relators are signed words in
$\{\pm1,\ldots,\pm n\}$, with $j$ denoting $g_j$.  Certificate records use
the monoid alphabet $L_n=\{1,\ldots,2n\}$ and the decoding map
\[
 \operatorname{dec}(2j-1)=j,\qquad \operatorname{dec}(2j)=-j.
\]
Thus the required inverse-letter table is
$(2,1,4,3,\ldots,2n,2n-1)$.  If $w$ is a signed word, write $\bar w$ for
its reverse with all signs changed, $\operatorname{fr}(w)$ for free
reduction, and $\operatorname{cr}(w)$ for the word obtained by additionally
removing inverse first/last pairs until none remain.  Define
\[
 \operatorname{ck}(w)=
 \min_{\mathrm{lex}}\{\text{cyclic rotations of }
   \operatorname{cr}(w)\text{ and }\overline{\operatorname{cr}(w)}\},
\]
with $\operatorname{ck}(1)=1$.  For certificate words $u,v$ put
\[
 E(u,v)=\operatorname{ck}\bigl(\operatorname{dec}(u)
   \overline{\operatorname{dec}(v)}\bigr).
\]
Equality of equation keys therefore means equality after free and cyclic
reduction, cyclic conjugacy, and inversion.  Each operation preserves the
normal closure of the equation word.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[literal rewrite trace]\label{def:trace}
Suppose records $R_0,\ldots,R_{i-1}$ have been accepted, with
$R_r=(\ell_r,\rho_r)$.  A trace on a word $w$ is a list of pairs $(r,p)$
with $r<i$ and $0\leq p\leq |w|$.  At that step the contiguous subword beginning at
$p$ must equal $\ell_r$ literally, and it is replaced by $\rho_r$.
No cyclic matching, inversion, or implicit free reduction occurs inside a
trace.  Write $T(w)$ for its final word.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[certificate grammar]\label{def:grammar}
A \texttt{luttinger-kbmag-proof-v1} certificate is a finite ordered list of
records $(\ell_i,\rho_i)$, together with source-binding metadata and identity
roots.  Every side is a word over $L_n$.  The case index must be an integer
in the range of the source filling array.  The source digest, case index and
slug, generator count, full relator list, and inverse table must agree
exactly with the hash-bound input.  A record has exactly one of four proof
forms.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.3em]
\item An \emph{inverse axiom} has $\rho_i=1$ and
  $\ell_i=a\,\iota(a)$ for the required inverse table $\iota$.

\item An \emph{input-relator record} names an in-range source relator $q_k$
  and satisfies $E(\ell_i,\rho_i)=\operatorname{ck}(q_k)$.

\item An \emph{overlap record} names parents $a,b<i$, an integer offset
  $d$ with $-|\ell_b|<d<|\ell_a|$, and two traces.  The two parent left
  sides must agree literally on the resulting nonempty overlap.  Let $w$ be
  the shortest word containing both occurrences.  Replacing the occurrence
  of $\ell_a$ by $\rho_a$ gives one branch $w_a$, and replacing
  $\ell_b$ by $\rho_b$ gives the other $w_b$.  If the recorded traces give
  $u=T_a(w_a)$ and $v=T_b(w_b)$, validity requires
  $E(\ell_i,\rho_i)=E(u,v)$.

\item A \emph{change record} names an old record $o<i$ and traces on its two
  sides.  Their literal outputs must equal the separately stored
  \texttt{reduced\_left} and \texttt{reduced\_right} words, say $u,v$, and
  validity requires $E(\ell_i,\rho_i)=E(u,v)$.
\end{enumerate}

Finally the root array has length exactly $2n$; its entry for each
$a\in L_n$ is an in-range record whose two sides are literally $(a,1)$.
Full-inventory mode also requires one correctly slugged certificate for each
of the eight source fillings, with no repeated index or slug and with the
stated presentation dimensions.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[certificate soundness]\label{thm:sound}
Let $G$ be the group defined by the source relators for a selected filling.
If a certificate satisfies Definitions~\ref{def:keys}--\ref{def:grammar},
then $G$ is trivial.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induct over the records.  An inverse axiom is an equality in the free group.
For an input record, the equation word is a freely and cyclically reduced
cyclic conjugate or inverse of a defining relator, so its two sides agree in
$G$.  Every trace replaces a subword by the other side of an earlier valid
equation and therefore preserves the represented element of $G$.

For an overlap, both untraced branches come from one source word by applying
one earlier equality, so they agree in $G$; their traces preserve that
equality.  Equality of equation keys transfers the resulting equality to
$(\ell_i,\rho_i)$.  For a change, the two sides of an earlier valid equation
are separately rewritten by earlier equations, and the same equation-key
argument applies.  Thus every accepted record is an equality in $G$.  The
root records say literally that every generator letter and its inverse equal
$1$, so $G=1$.
\end{proof}

Definitions~\ref{def:keys}--\ref{def:grammar} are the normative mathematical
specification.  The complete reference control flow and concrete file format
are reproduced in the supplement and in
\repopath{verification/luttinger/CERTIFICATE_SPEC.md}.  The Python and Ruby
checkers separately implement it using only their standard libraries.  They
share neither source code nor runtime, but both were produced within this
project and have not received an independent human line-by-line audit.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
For each of the four selected entries, the certificate bundle prepared with
this version contains a certificate bound to the manifest-listed source digest,
case index, slug, complete
relator list, generator count, and inverse table.  The Python and Ruby

\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[human-readable terminal collapse]
\label{prop:terminal-collapse}
Write the three generators of the fixed $(+,+)$ presentation as $a,b,c$.
Three accepted certificate equations imply the more readable consequences
\[
       a=bc,\qquad ac^2=1,\qquad b^2c=cab.
\]
These three consequences already force $a=b=c=1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Records $6649$, $6662$, and $6665$ of the zero-based ancestry array are,
literally,
\[
 ac^{-1}=b,\qquad c^{-2}=a,\qquad c^{-1}b^2=abc^{-1}.
\]
Right multiplication of the first by $c$, right multiplication of the second
by $c^2$, and left multiplication of the third by $c$ followed by right
multiplication by $c$ give the three displayed consequences.  Now $a=bc$ and
$ac^2=1$ imply $bc^3=1$, so $b=c^{-3}$ and $a=c^{-2}$.  Substitution into
$b^2c=cab$ gives $c^{-5}=c^{-4}$, hence $c=1$, and then $a=b=1$.  The certificate carries the
long proof that the three short equations follow from the original eighty
relators; this paragraph exposes the final algebraic reason the group dies.
\end{proof}

The finite theorem is therefore relative only to its published mathematical
specification and to the proposition that at least one implementation
conforms to it.  The rest of the paper asks the different question: what
geometric object do the hash-bound presentations describe?  The fixed
$(+,+)$ presentation is identified with $V_{\mathrm{aud}}$ below; comparison
with Wuebben is postponed to Section~\ref{sec:relation}.  The
supplement records the complete implementation inventory and the detailed
layer-by-layer trust boundary.

\paragraph{What the three generators mean.}
The three generators of $Q$ are not privileged loops drawn in the
surface-bundle picture.  They are the survivors of a proof-producing Tietze
transport that starts with one edge generator for each non-tree edge of the
drilled simplicial exterior.  The seventy-eight relators retain the full
two-skeleton information after those eliminations.  Geometric meaning is
carried separately by eighty-nine tracked words: the original fiber and base
loops, both meridians, both product longitudes, and the comparison paths are
all expressed in the three survivors.  Thus $Q$ is a compact algebraic
coordinate system for the torus exterior, while the tracked words are the
dictionary back to its geometry.  The proof below never assigns a geometric
name to a survivor merely because it simplifies a relation.

\section{Geometric identification of the audit manifold}
\label{sec:target}\label{sec:model}

This section proves that the $(+,+)$ finite presentation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a presentation of the explicitly defined
$V_{\mathrm{aud}}$.  The other three sign sheets remain algebraic robustness
checks.  This section makes no comparison with the target paper's
denotation; that logically separate problem is discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:relation}.

The genus-2 fiber is realized as a regular neighborhood of the specified
five-chain of curves --- five plumbed annular bands and two cone discs
--- with the hyperelliptic-type involution $\varphi_0$ realized
simplicially; its fixed points, the curve actions $a\leftrightarrow e$,
$b\leftrightarrow d$, and the free half-rotation of the middle curve $c$
are executable assertions about the complex, not assumptions. The bundle
$R_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is assembled from simplicial mapping cylinders over the once-punctured
torus base; the two surgery tori $T_\alpha$, $T_\beta$ arise as induced
subcomplexes, and the assembled 4-complex passes manifold checks down to
the vertex-link level.

The finite artifact inventory and checker acceptance results are kept in the
supplement.  They establish finite predicates only.  The lemmas below supply
the mathematical conversions from simplicial incidences, paths, and word
lists to the stated topological conclusions; successful replay is not used as
a substitute for those conversions.

\begin{lemma}[equivariant marked fiber]\label{lem:fiber}
There is an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
$h:|F_K|\to F$ carrying the ordered curves $(a,b,c,d,e)$ and marked points
$(p,O)$ to those of Definition~\ref{def:Vaudit} and satisfying
$h\varphi_K=\varphi_0h$.  It carries the named based loops $x,y,r,s$ with
their displayed orientations.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Finite evidence.}  The independent ribbon reconstruction has
\[
 i(a,b)=i(b,c)=i(c,d)=i(d,e)=1,
 \qquad i(u,v)=0\quad\text{otherwise}.
\]
Its regular neighborhood retracts to a graph with six vertices and ten
edges, hence has Euler characteristic $-4$.  The two disjoint symplectic
pairs $(a,b)$ and $(d,e)$ force genus two, so the neighborhood has two
boundary components.  The complement in the certified genus-two surface
has Euler characteristic two and exactly those two boundary circles; it is
therefore two disks.  An exhaustive rotation-system check begins with the
$36$ cyclic orders at the independent four-valent crossings.  Alternation,
orientation preservation, two boundary faces, and preservation of the two
faces leave exactly four systems; reversing the permitted curve
orientations normalizes all four to one equivariant oriented ribbon type.
The primary and independent fibers realize that same labeled type, including
the involution on every directed curve end and the two $p/O$ faces.

\emph{Topological input.}  Map vertex disks and edge bands by the checked
cyclic correspondence.  These product maps agree on attaching intervals,
so they give an equivariant homeomorphism of the ribbon neighborhoods.
Each complementary disk is invariant: if the involution exchanged them it
would have no fixed point there, contrary to the certified two-point fixed
set.  The periodic-disk classification
\cite[Theorem~3.1 and Proposition~3.2]{ConstantinKolev} identifies each
nontrivial orientation-preserving order-two disk action with a half-turn and
shows that a periodic disk map fixed on the boundary is the identity.  More
explicitly, quotient each complementary disk by its half-turn.  Both
quotients are disks with one marked branch point, and the already constructed
equivariant boundary map descends to their boundary circles.  The Alexander
extension, chosen to carry branch point to branch point, lifts uniquely after
one boundary lift is fixed; the lift is equivariant and agrees with the ribbon
map on the boundary.  This gives the required equivariant marked map relative
to the boundary.  The based path comparison
then reads, literally,
\[
 \varphi_0:(x,y,r,s)\longmapsto(r,s,x,y).
\]
No fundamental-group filling or downstream theorem enters this argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[relative monodromy and graph clutching]\label{lem:clutching}
The map $h$ can be chosen so that it conjugates the two monodromy
representatives relative to the full $c$ and $e$ collars.  It consequently
extends to an orientation-preserving fiberwise homeomorphism
\[
 H:|K|\longrightarrow (R_{\mathrm{aud}})_{\mathrm{top}}
\]
carrying the basepoint, both torus subbundles, and their product collars to
the corresponding marked objects.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Finite evidence.}  The literal based actions are
\[
 \varphi_0:(x,y,r,s)\mapsto(r,s,x,y),\qquad
 \psi_0:(x,y,r,s)\mapsto(y^{-1},yx,r,s).
\]
The second action is obtained from complete whiskered paths, not free
homotopy classes, and is replayed in two differently subdivided beta stacks.
On the three-row $c$ collar the alpha map is exactly the free shift by four
columns.  The beta representative is the ordered supported word
$T_a\circ T_b$, with $T_b$ first; every trace cell avoids the full
$e$ collar, and the restricted tetrahedra form exactly its product staircase.

\emph{Topological conversion.}  Both bundle models are the union of a
common fiber block and two mapping cylinders over the rank-two spine of
$\Sigma_{1,1}$; there is no base two-cell.  For a monodromy $f$, write
\[
 M_f=(F\times[0,1])/((z,1)\sim(f(z),0)).
\]
If $hf_K=f_*h$, the formula $[z,t]\mapsto[h(z),t]$ respects the seam and
has the analogous inverse.  For an isotopic target representative, the
standard interpolating formula
$[z,t]\mapsto[k_t(z),t]$, with
$f_*k_1=k_0f_K$, gives the same conclusion.  The alpha conjugacy is exact
on the $c$ collar.  For beta, both sides use the identical supported twist
word; interpolating twist profiles is relative to the disjoint $e$ collar.
The two handle maps restrict to $h$ on their common fiber and therefore
glue, as do their inverses.  This constructs $H$ directly; bundle
classification and Dehn--Nielsen--Baer are independent controls, not proof
inputs.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[the marked torus pair]\label{cor:tori}
Under $H$, the induced subcomplexes
$c\rtimes_{\varphi_K}S^1_\alpha$ and $e\times S^1_\beta$ map to
$T_\alpha,T_\beta$, together with their product collars and the $p$-section.
All smooth and symplectic arguments may therefore be performed in the
audit-defined smooth target $R_{\mathrm{aud}}$; no smoothing of $|K|$ is chosen.
\end{corollary}

The model results through Corollary~\ref{cor:tori} concern $V_{\mathrm{aud}}$
and use no source-comparison clause.  Five structurally different
constructions test subdivision, monodromy order, twist sign, labels, and
orientations.  These internal controls support the audit object; they do not
identify it with the object denoted by a source diagram.  That open
comparison is discussed only in Section~\ref{sec:relation}.

\section{Peripheral data and the product filling}\label{sec:peripheral}

Let $T=T_\alpha\sqcup T_\beta$.  Transport the explicit normal block
neighborhood $N_{1/2}(T)$ below through the marked homeomorphism $H$, and
use the resulting product-collared neighborhood as $\nu(T)$.  Put
$C_{\mathrm{aud}}=R_{\mathrm{aud}}\smallsetminus\operatorname{int}\nu(T)$.
This section identifies
the normal boundary of each component, its commonly based peripheral pair,
and the two filling slopes.  The statements are deliberately separated so
that a failure of one cannot be hidden by triviality of the final groups.
This section proves the intrinsic product-filling theorem; it uses no
symplectic or Lagrangian-framing input.

\subsection{Normal frontier and compact exterior}

\begin{lemma}[local flatness and the normal frontier]\label{lem:normal}
The two torus subcomplexes are disjoint, full, and locally flat.  The
barycentric level set
\[
 N_{1/2}(T)=\left\{x:\sum_{v\in T}\lambda_v(x)\geq\tfrac12\right\}
\]
is an explicit normal $D^2$-block bundle, and its frontier
$F_{1/2}(T)$ is the normal-circle boundary.  The derived frontier used by
the computation is PL homeomorphic to $F_{1/2}(T)$, carrying every extracted
dual loop to a literal oriented normal-circle fiber.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every simplex $\sigma\subset T$, the complete link pair is checked to
be
\[
 (S^3,\text{unknotted }S^1),\quad(S^2,S^0),\quad
 (S^1,\varnothing)
\]
in dimensions $0,1,2$, respectively.  The enumeration covers every simplex
of both tori.  Vertex-link $3$-spheres and
unknotted link circles carry independently replayed collapse and cyclic-knot
witnesses.  Since the star pair of a simplex is the join of that simplex with
its link pair, these standard links give the required standard local chart at
every point.  This is the simplex-link formulation of PL local flatness; see
\cite[Chapter~4, pp.~50--51]{RourkeSanderson}.

In a simplex with torus face $A$ and opposite face $B$, barycentric
coordinates give the unique block form
\[
 N_{1/2}(T)\cap\sigma\cong\Delta_A\times C(\Delta_B),
 \qquad F_{1/2}(T)\cap\sigma\cong\Delta_A\times\Delta_B.
\]
The formulas agree on common faces.  If $b(s)$ is a mixed-simplex vertex of
the computed derived frontier, the map
\[
 b(s)\longmapsto\tfrac12b(s\cap T)+\tfrac12b(s\smallsetminus T)
\]
extends cellwise to the canonical subdivision map for
$\Delta_A\times\Delta_B$ and hence gives the global PL homeomorphism.  Every
mixed vertex and derived comparability is checked.  For every torus triangle the alternating dual loop has
constant torus coordinate and is precisely the subdivided normal link
circle.  No regular-neighborhood uniqueness or smoothing theorem is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[complement presentation]\label{lem:complement}
Let $L=K[V(K)\smallsetminus V(T)]$ be the full subcomplex on the vertices
outside $T$.  There is a homotopy-equivalence diagram
\[
 |L|\ \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\
 |K|\smallsetminus\operatorname{int}N_{1/2}(T)\
 \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\ |K|\smallsetminus|T|,
 \qquad
 |K|\smallsetminus\operatorname{int}N_{1/2}(T)\cong C_{\mathrm{aud}},
\]
in which both spaces on the right strongly deformation retract onto $|L|$.
Consequently the maximal-tree edge generators of $L$ and its oriented
triangle boundaries give a presentation of
$\pione(C_{\mathrm{aud}})$.  A sealed Tietze certificate reduces the raw
cellular presentation to the three-generator, seventy-eight-relator
presentation $Q$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, while transporting all named
peripheral and comparison words.  Exact cellular and transport counts are in
the supplement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On each simplex not contained in the full subcomplex $T$, let $r(x)$ be the
point obtained by setting the coordinates on $T$ to zero and renormalizing
the remaining barycentric coordinates.  The straight-line homotopy
$H_t(x)=(1-t)x+tr(x)$ agrees on faces and fixes $|L|$.  If
$\rho(x)=\sum_{v\in T}\lambda_v(x)$, then
$\rho(H_t(x))=(1-t)\rho(x)$.  Thus $H_t$ stays in
$|K|\smallsetminus|T|$ and also restricts to the block exterior
$\{\rho\leq1/2\}=|K|\smallsetminus\operatorname{int}N_{1/2}(T)$.
The homeomorphism $H$ and the chosen product collar identify that block
exterior with $C_{\mathrm{aud}}$.  This proves all three asserted homotopy
identifications, including the previously implicit passage from the deleted
subcomplex to the compact torus exterior.

Collapsing a maximal tree and applying cellular van Kampen to every oriented
two-simplex gives the raw presentation.  Each recorded Tietze elimination is
replayed against the unique current relator that implies it; separate Python
and Ruby checker implementations agree on the final presentation and every
tracked word.
\end{proof}

Based meridians and product-framing longitudes for both tori are traced as
literal simplicial loops carrying the audit definition's basing whiskers.
External consistency checks compare the resulting words with Wuebben's
corrected relation sheet and basing convention, but those comparisons play
no logical role in any theorem about $V_{\mathrm{aud}}$.  Internally, a
combinatorial sweep of the relevant transport square resolves the basing
double-count question from the audit paths themselves.  Both surgery tori are certified locally
flat at every simplex link, and every extracted dual meridian is checked
to be a literal normal-circle fiber of an explicit normal block model,
with no regular-neighborhood theorem invoked.  The doubled-section datum
used in the historical target application is kept in the supplement; the
intrinsic section of $V_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is treated directly below.

\subsection{Relation sheet and product fillings}

To make Theorem~\ref{prop:ident} inspectable without opening the
repository, fix the orientation sheet used by the audit triangulation and put
$\delta=r^{-1}$.  Before filling, the resulting coordinate presentation read
from the literal paths has the surface relation, the following eight
transport relations, and the drilled-fiber relation:
\begin{align*}
AxA^{-1}&=r, & AyA^{-1}&=s, & ArA^{-1}&=x,
& AsA^{-1}&=Ny,\\
BxB^{-1}&=y^{-1}, & ByB^{-1}&=M^{-1}yx,
& BrB^{-1}&=r, & BsB^{-1}&=(r^{-1}M^{-1}r)s,\\
[x,y][r,s]&=1, &&
B(s^{-1}r^{-1}yx)B^{-1}&=r^{-1}s^{-1}x.
\end{align*}
The two commonly based product-framed directions are
\[
 \lambda_\alpha=Ax,\qquad
 \lambda_\beta=(r^{-1}Mr)B,
\]
and the four algebraic sign sheets append exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fillings}
 M\lambda_\alpha^{\epsilon_A}=1,\qquad
 N\lambda_\beta^{\epsilon_B}=1,\qquad
 (\epsilon_A,\epsilon_B)\in\{\pm1\}^2.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[abelianization of a slope family]
\label{prop:slope-family-h1}
For $p,q\in\Z$, let $P_{p,q}$ be the quotient of the sealed complement
presentation $Q$ obtained by adjoining
\[
             M\lambda_\alpha^p=1,\qquad
             N\lambda_\beta^q=1.
\]
With the convention $\Z/0=\Z$,
\[
             H_1(P_{p,q};\Z)\cong \Z/q\oplus\Z/p.
\]
In particular the complement has infinitely many fillings with nontrivial
fundamental group.  Omitting either filling relation leaves an infinite
cyclic summand, while $|p|>1$ or $|q|>1$ produces nonzero torsion in
abelianization.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $g_1,g_2,g_3$ be the generators of $Q$ in its sealed order.  The exponent
vectors of its $78$ relators span exactly
$\Z(0,0,1)\subset\Z^3$, so
\[
             H_1(Q;\Z)=\Z\langle g_1\rangle\oplus
                        \Z\langle g_2\rangle.
\]
The hash-bound tracked words have exponent vectors
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &M&N&\lambda_\alpha&\lambda_\beta\\ \hline
\text{vector in }\Z^3 &(0,0,1)&(0,0,0)&(0,1,2)&(-1,0,-3).
\end{array}
\]
Thus their classes in $H_1(Q)$ are respectively
$0,0,g_2,-g_1$.  The two new relations impose
$pg_2=0$ and $qg_1=0$, which gives the displayed answer.  The single-filling
and nonunit-coefficient assertions follow immediately.
\end{proof}

These are four algebraic specializations in one fixed symbolic basis.  Only
the $(+,+)$ specialization is used below as a presentation of
$V_{\mathrm{aud}}$.  The finite presentations of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} are
the frozen Tietze images of all four literal word lists; for the other three,
no identification with a filled geometric manifold is asserted here.

\begin{lemma}[extension across a torus filling]\label{lem:torus-extension}
Let $X=T^2\times D^2$ and order an integral basis of its boundary as
$(\mu,\gamma_1,\gamma_2)$, where $\mu$ bounds the disk fiber.  A boundary
diffeomorphism $f:\partial X\to\partial X'$ extends over $X$ if and only if
$f_*(\mu)=\epsilon\mu'$ for $\epsilon\in\{\pm1\}$.  In these bases its
matrix then has the form
\[
 [f_*]=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \epsilon & u_1 & u_2\\
  0 & a_{11} & a_{12}\\
  0 & a_{21} & a_{22}
 \end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&u\\0&A\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad A\in GL(2,\mathbb Z),\quad u\in\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Consequently a diffeomorphism of torus exteriors that carries each killed
attaching slope to the corresponding slope up to sign extends across the
filled pieces; the two surviving boundary directions are recorded by
$A$ and $u$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Necessity follows because the kernel of
$H_1(\partial X)\to H_1(X)$ is the primitive subgroup generated by $\mu$.
For sufficiency, write $x\in\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2$ for the base coordinate
and $z\in D^2\subset\mathbb C$ for the disk coordinate.  Up to translations,
the displayed matrix is realized by
\[
 (x,z)\longmapsto
 \begin{cases}
  (Ax,e^{2\pi i\langle u,x\rangle}z),&\epsilon=+1,\\
  (Ax,e^{2\pi i\langle u,x\rangle}\overline z),&\epsilon=-1.
 \end{cases}
\]
This restricts to the required affine isotopy class on the boundary.  The
linearization theorem for the flat three-torus
\cite[case~(IV), pp.~464--465]{HatcherLinearization} says that every
diffeomorphism of $T^3$ is isotopic to an affine representative; its linear
part is the induced action on integral first homology.  Extending that isotopy
through a boundary collar adjusts the displayed extension to $f$.

The orientation sign of the displayed four-dimensional map is
$\epsilon\det A$, which is also $\det[f_*]$ on the ordered boundary basis.
Thus an orientation-preserving boundary map has an orientation-preserving
extension, and an orientation-reversing boundary map has an
orientation-reversing extension.  In the filled-exterior application one
conjugates the exterior boundary map by the two attaching maps.  If both
attaching maps reverse the boundary orientation, their two signs cancel, so
the conjugated map has the same orientation sign as the exterior map.  This
reduces the extension problem to the same meridian criterion and accounts
explicitly for the third boundary direction as well as the orientation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[peripheral convention changes]\label{lem:sign-conventions}
Let $(\mu,\lambda,\nu)$ be an oriented integral basis of the boundary
three-torus of one deleted torus, with geometric filling slope
$s=\mu+\lambda$.  Reversing the orientations used to name both $\mu$ and
$\lambda$, while leaving $\nu$ fixed, is the coordinate change
\[
 D_- = \begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\0&-1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}
 \in SL(3,\mathbb Z).
\]
It carries $s$ to $-s$ and hence preserves the unoriented filling slope.
The corresponding boundary map extends across the filling
$T^2\times D^2$.  In the commonly whiskered peripheral subgroup, the same
relation is written
\[
 (\mu\lambda)^{-1}=\lambda^{-1}\mu^{-1}
   =\mu^{-1}\lambda^{-1}=1,
\]
where the second equality uses commutativity of the peripheral
$\pione(T^3)$.
By contrast, reversing only the displayed longitude changes the slope to
$\mu-\lambda$; the matrix
$\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1)\in SL(3,\mathbb Z)$ sends $s$ to that distinct
primitive class.  It is not a convention change preserving the fixed
filling unless an additional self-diffeomorphism of the complement carrying
$\mu+\lambda$ to $\mu-\lambda$ is supplied.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The two displayed matrices give the stated changes on
$H_1(T^3;\mathbb Z)$.  Since $s$ and $-s$ define the same primitive kernel,
Lemma~\ref{lem:torus-extension} extends the first coordinate change over the
filling piece.  Because the meridian and longitude use a common whisker,
their boundary subgroup is abelian; hence inversion of
$\mu\lambda$ gives the displayed word.  Finally $(1,-1,0)$ is not
$\pm(1,1,0)$, proving the last assertion.  The same argument applies
independently on the alpha and beta boundary components.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the alpha peripheral pair]\label{lem:alpha-peripheral}
Under the normal-frontier identification of Lemma~\ref{lem:normal}, the
extracted loops are the commonly based meridian and product longitude
$(M,\lambda_\alpha)$, and
\[
 \lambda_\alpha=Ax\quad\text{in }\pione(C_{\mathrm{aud}}).
\]
The displayed equality is certified using only the $78$ complement relators,
with neither filling relation present.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Consider $T_\alpha$.  The curve $c$ meets the oriented $y$ edge once, at
$c_y$.  Starting at $p$, the stored path follows the initial segment $y_1$
to $c_y$, makes a normal jog to the frontier of the explicit normal block,
and traverses the oriented dual normal circle.  Returning along the same
whisker gives $M$.  The longitude uses that identical whisker and normal
jog, traverses the base direction $A$, and closes along that same half of
$c$, from $c_s=\varphi_0(c_y)$ to $c_y$.  These two literal based paths are
what $M$ and $\lambda_\alpha$ denote throughout and what the finite
presentation fills with.

That the two loops use the same whisker is not an inference: in the sealed
transport input they are
$y_1\mu_\alpha y_1^{-1}$ and
$y_1\lambda_\alpha^{\circ}y_1^{-1}$ with the identical whisker $y_1$.
So no independent conjugation is
hidden in the filling word
$M\lambda_\alpha^{\epsilon_A}$.

The identification of the extracted longitude with $Ax$ has two independent
supports.  Geometrically it follows from how
the path is built --- based on the $y$ rail at the unique crossing $c_y$,
whiskered by $y_1$, jogged to the normal-block frontier, lifting the base
generator $A$, and closed along the half of $c$ from $c_s$ to $c_y$.  Algebraically,
the elementary elimination pass over the sealed presentation leaves a
nonempty residual for $\lambda_\alpha^{-1}Ax$, but a proof-producing
completion reduces that exact word to the identity.  Independent Python and
Ruby verifiers replay its complete ancestry cone from the sealed
three-generator, $78$-relator complement; neither filling relation is
present.  The supplement gives the exact path identifiers, word lengths,

\begin{theorem}[audit-model identification]\label{prop:ident}
For the fixed audit sheet $(\epsilon_A,\epsilon_B)=(+1,+1)$ the natural map

No geometric identification of $P_{+,-},

\begin{proof}
Lemmas~\ref{lem:fiber} and \ref{lem:clutching} construct the marked bundle
homeomorphism and carry the two curve subbundles and their product collars to

\ref{lem:beta-peripheral} identify the two commonly based product

beta sweep.  Lemma~\ref{lem:drilled} places the final transport relation in
the drilled complement.

By Definition~\ref{def:Vaudit}, attaching the two product-framed filling

 \big\langle\!\big\langle
 M\lambda_\alpha,
 N\lambda_\beta
 \big\rangle\!\big\rangle.
\]
Cellular van Kampen therefore gives a surjection from the filled
presentation.  Lemma~\ref{lem:complement} gives an actual presentation of
the complement, not merely a list of valid relations; applying van Kampen

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:audit-manifold}, except symplecticity]
Use the $(+,+)$ sheet in Theorem~\ref{prop:ident}.  Its source is trivial
by Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; a quotient of the trivial
group is trivial.  Notice that this conclusion needs only surjectivity in
Theorem~\ref{prop:ident}, no symplectic or Lagrangian-framing result, and
no source-identification assumption.

The boundary of $R_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is the $F$-bundle over
$\partial\Sigma_{1,1}\cong S^1$ whose monodromy is the commutator determined
by the oriented boundary word in the two clutching maps.  It is connected.
Both torus surgeries take place in the interior, so this is also
$\partial V_{\mathrm{aud}}$.  Since
\[
 \chi(F)=2-2\cdot2=-2,\qquad
 \chi(\Sigma_{1,1})=2-2-1=-1,
\]
multiplicativity for the bundle gives
$\chi(R_{\mathrm{aud}})=2$.  Removing and regluing two copies of
$T^2\times D^2$, each with Euler characteristic zero and boundary $T^3$,
does not change Euler characteristic.  Hence
$\chi(V_{\mathrm{aud}})=2$.

Simple connectivity gives $H_1(V_{\mathrm{aud}};\Z)=0$.  Because both the
manifold and its boundary are connected, the beginning of the cohomology
sequence of the pair, together with $H^1(V_{\mathrm{aud}};\Z)=0$, gives
$H^1(V_{\mathrm{aud}},\partial V_{\mathrm{aud}};\Z)=0$.
Lefschetz duality therefore gives $H_3(V_{\mathrm{aud}};\Z)=0$.
Similarly the homology sequence of the pair gives
$H_1(V_{\mathrm{aud}},\partial V_{\mathrm{aud}};\Z)=0$; duality and the
universal coefficient theorem then show that $H_2(V_{\mathrm{aud}};\Z)$ is
torsion-free.  These are the cases of the universal coefficient theorem and
Lefschetz duality stated in \cite[Theorems~3.2 and~3.43]{HatcherAT}.
Since the boundary is nonempty, $H_4=0$, and the Euler characteristic now
forces $H_2\cong\Z$.

Choose a base point in the interior of the base away from the two clutching
loops.  Its fiber $F$ misses both surgery tori, survives the fillings, and
has trivial normal bundle, hence $F^2=0$.  The fixed point $p$, which lies
on neither $c$ nor $e$, gives a proper section
$\Gamma\cong\Sigma_{1,1}$ disjoint from the surgery tori.  It also survives,
and $F\cdot\Gamma=1$.  Thus $[F]$ is primitive and generates $H_2$.
Modulo two, $H_2$ is generated by $[F]$.  On $F$ the oriented splitting
$TV_{\mathrm{aud}}|_F=TF\oplus\nu F$, the Whitney product formula, and
the mod-two reduction of the two Euler classes give
\[
 \langle w_2(V_{\mathrm{aud}}),[F]\rangle
   =\chi(F)+F^2=-2+0=0\pmod2.
\]
See \cite[\S\S4 and~9]{MilnorStasheff}.  Since evaluation on $[F]$
detects $H^2(V_{\mathrm{aud}};\Z/2)$, it follows that
$w_2(V_{\mathrm{aud}})=0$, so $V_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is spin.  The remaining
symplectic assertion is proved after Theorem~\ref{thm:framing}.
\end{proof}

\section{The product-to-Lagrangian framing bridge}\label{sec:framing}

The proof of simple connectivity and the intrinsic topological assertions is
complete before this section begins.  The framing theorem is not used in
Theorem~\ref{prop:ident}.  It proves the source-independent symplectic
assertion of Theorem~\ref{thm:audit-manifold}; it is also the relevant bridge
in any future comparison with a Lagrangian source construction.

\begin{lemma}[bundle symplectic form and protected collars]
\label{lem:bundle-symplectic-form}
The monodromy representatives in Definition~\ref{def:Vaudit} may be chosen
to preserve one positive area form $\Omega$ on $F$, with $\psi_0$ equal to
the identity on a collar of $e$ and $\varphi_0$ equal to the stated
half-rotation on a collar of $c$.  Write
$\pi:R_{\mathrm{aud}}\to\Sigma_{1,1}$ for the bundle projection.  If $\eta$
is a positive area form on $\Sigma_{1,1}$, then for every $K>0$
\[
             \omega_K=\Omega_{\mathrm{vert}}+K\pi^*\eta
\]
is a closed symplectic form on $R_{\mathrm{aud}}$.  The two tori
$T_\alpha,T_\beta$ are Lagrangian.  On disjoint protected neighborhoods of
them there are oriented coordinates
$(\theta_1,t,\theta_2,u)$ in which
\[
 \omega_K=f(\theta_1,t)\,dt\wedge d\theta_1
             +K\,du\wedge d\theta_2,
 \qquad f>0,
\]
with no mixed vertical--horizontal terms.  On the alpha neighborhood these
coordinates obey the seam
$(\theta_1,t,2\pi,u)\sim(\theta_1+\pi,t,0,u)$; on the beta neighborhood they
are ordinary product coordinates.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Average any positive area form over the order-two action of $\varphi_0$.
An equivariant tubular coordinate on the invariant curve $c$ then writes
$\varphi_0(\theta,t)=(\theta+\pi,t)$ and the averaged form as
$f(\theta,t)dt\wedge d\theta$.  For this same area form, represent $T_a$
and $T_b$ by the usual area-preserving annular shears.  Because $e$ is
disjoint from $a$ and $b$, their supports may be chosen disjoint from a
protected collar of $e$; hence $\psi_0=T_a\circ T_b$ preserves $\Omega$ and
is the identity on that collar.  Thus both clutching maps preserve one area
form and retain the required collar behavior.

Use the flat product atlas determined by the two locally constant clutching
maps.  The fiber form descends in that atlas to a globally defined closed
vertical form $\Omega_{\mathrm{vert}}$: on overlaps the transition is
locally constant in the base and preserves $\Omega$.  Therefore
$d\omega_K=0$, and, with the fiber and base orientations fixed by
$\Omega$ and $\eta$,
\[
       \omega_K^2=2K\,\Omega_{\mathrm{vert}}\wedge\pi^*\eta>0.
\]
This proves symplecticity and fixes the total orientation.  Each surgery
torus has one fiber tangent and one base tangent, so both summands restrict
to zero on it.

Choose $\eta=du\wedge d\theta_2$ on disjoint annular collars of the two base
loops and write $\Omega=f(\theta_1,t)dt\wedge d\theta_1$ on the protected
fiber collars.  The flat atlas then gives the displayed split expressions;
there are no mixed terms to remove.  The identity behavior of $\psi_0$ gives
the beta product chart, while the half-rotation of $\varphi_0$ gives exactly
the alpha seam.  These choices can be made simultaneously because the
protected collars are disjoint.  We orient the normal momentum plane by
$(t,Ku)$; its boundary orientation is the positive meridian convention used
below.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Lagrangian-framing class]
\label{def:lagrangian-framing}
Let $T$ be either audit torus with its oriented product coordinates, and let
$\gamma\subset T$ be one of the named primitive curves.  In a Weinstein
chart fixing the zero section, take a sufficiently small nonzero constant
covector copy of $\gamma$ and radially project it to
$\partial\nu(T)$.  Its isotopy class in
$\nu(T)\smallsetminus T$ is the chartwise Lagrangian push-off class.  The
chart-independence part of Theorem~\ref{thm:framing} proves that this class is
independent of the Weinstein chart and of the sufficiently small constant
covector; the resulting boundary class is what we call the canonical
Lagrangian-framing class of $\gamma$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[product framing equals Lagrangian framing]
\label{thm:framing}
For the symplectic form $\omega_K$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:bundle-symplectic-form}, the product-framed
push-offs in Lemmas~\ref{lem:alpha-peripheral} and
\ref{lem:beta-peripheral} represent the canonical Lagrangian-framing classes
of $T_\alpha$ and $T_\beta$.  In particular neither acquires a meridian
component when transported into a Weinstein neighborhood.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof takes place in the already smooth target $R_{\mathrm{aud}}$.  By
Corollary~\ref{cor:tori}, the marked homeomorphism $H$ transports the product
collars and their push-offs from the PL model into that target.  No smooth
structure is placed on the source complex.  A framing push-off below means
the isotopy class, in $\nu(T_i)\smallsetminus T_i$, of a sufficiently small
copy of a curve on $T_i$, as in
Definition~\ref{def:lagrangian-framing}.

\emph{Relative normal form.}  On an annular fiber collar write
\[
 \Omega=f(\theta,t)\,dt\wedge d\theta,\qquad f>0,
\]
with core $t=0$.  Put
\[
 g(\theta,t)=\int_0^t(f(\theta,u)-1)\,du,\qquad
 H_s(\theta,t)=t+s g(\theta,t).
\]
Then $\partial_tH_s=(1-s)+sf>0$.  On one collar common to the compact core,
$H_s$ therefore has a smooth inverse in the radial coordinate.  If
$H_s(\theta,T_s(\theta,t_0))=t_0$, then $T_0=t_0$, $T_s(\theta,0)=0$, and
\[
 \partial_sT_s=-\frac{g(\theta,T_s)}{(1-s)+sf(\theta,T_s)}.
\]
Thus $(\theta,t_0)\mapsto(\theta,T_s)$ is the relative Moser isotopy, obtained
by monotone inversion rather than an abstract flow theorem.  At $s=1$,
differentiation in $t_0$ gives
$f(\theta,T_1)\partial_{t_0}T_1=1$, hence
$T_1^*\Omega=dt_0\wedge d\theta$.  The exact identities, fixed-core
condition, and a uniform collar bound have a finite symbolic replay listed
in the supplement.
Apply this construction independently on the disjoint protected $c$ and $e$
collars.  It therefore standardizes both neighborhoods simultaneously while
fixing both torus cores and leaving the form unchanged outside slightly
larger disjoint collars.

For the alpha collar the deck involution is
$\tau(\theta,t)=(\theta+\pi,t)$ and the quotient coordinate is
$\bar\theta=2\theta$; write $\pi_\alpha$ for the quotient map.  The preceding radial map is defined directly upstairs
by replacing $\bar\theta$ with $2\theta$ in its coefficient.  Periodicity
makes it $\tau$-equivariant, so no covering-lift choice is involved.  Moreover
\[
 \pi_\alpha^*(dt\wedge d\bar\theta)=2\,dt\wedge d\theta
   =d(2t)\wedge d\theta,
\]
which is the required factor-two normalization.  The corresponding
equivariance identities are replayed in the supplement's framing inventory.

\emph{The two charts.}  Near $T_\beta$, $\psi_0$ is the identity near $e$,
and the preceding normalization gives
\[
 \omega=dt\wedge d\theta_1+K\,du\wedge d\theta_2,\qquad K>0;
\]
the cotangent momenta are $(t,Ku)$.  The in-fiber and base-normal push-offs
are therefore constant-momentum copies.  Near $T_\alpha$, the mapping-torus
seam is
\[
 (\theta_1,t,2\pi,u)\sim(\theta_1+\pi,t,0,u).
\]
On its quotient put
\[
 \Theta_1=\theta_1-\tfrac12\theta_2,\quad
 \Theta_2=\theta_2,\quad P_1=t,\quad P_2=\tfrac12t+Ku.
\]
These functions respect the seam and satisfy
\[
 dP_1\wedge d\Theta_1+dP_2\wedge d\Theta_2
 =dt\wedge d\theta_1+K\,du\wedge d\theta_2.
\]
The fiber push-off has momentum $(t_0,t_0/2)$ and the half-drifting base
push-off has momentum $(0,Ku_0)$, both constant.  Hence neither acquires a
meridian component, which is precisely the equality of framings used in
\eqref{eq:fillings}.  The Liouville primitive
$t\,d\theta_1+Ku\,d\theta_2$ forces these momenta by pairing with the
$\Theta$-frame; the supplement records an independent replay of that
derivation, the seam, exterior algebra, and a second opposite-shear chart.

Here is the boundary-class assertion in coordinates.  Let $\gamma$ be either
named curve, let $\eta$ complete it to an oriented basis of the zero-section
torus, and let $\mu$ be the positively oriented circle in a cotangent fiber.
After radial projection to the unit-covector boundary, a constant nonzero
covector $p_0$ gives
\[
 s\longmapsto \bigl(\gamma(s),p_0/\lVert p_0\rVert\bigr).
\]
Its normal-circle coordinate is constant, so its class in the boundary basis
$(\gamma,\eta,\mu)$ has $\mu$-coefficient zero.  This is the precise sense in
which ``constant covector'' excludes a meridional component.  The seam map
and the quotient map above preserve the displayed orientations: their
pullbacks give the positive forms written there, and the chosen positive
normal circle is the boundary orientation of the ordered momentum plane.

\emph{Independence of the Weinstein chart.}  It remains to show that the
constant-momentum conclusion is a framing class rather than a feature of
the displayed coordinates.  Given two Lagrangian-neighborhood charts, first
compose with a cotangent lift so that their restrictions to the zero section
agree.  Their transition is then a symplectomorphism germ
$\Phi:(T^*T,T)\to(T^*T,T)$ fixing the zero section pointwise.  With fiber
dilation $\delta_r(q,p)=(q,rp)$, define
\[
 \Phi_r=\delta_r^{-1}\Phi\delta_r\quad(0<r\leq1).
\]
Although $\delta_r^*\omega=r\omega$, the two factors cancel, so every $\Phi_r$
is symplectic.  Along the zero section the symplectic condition gives
\[
 d\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}I&A\\0&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad A=A^{\mathrm T}.
\]
Taylor expansion shows that $\Phi_r$ extends at $r=0$ by the identity.  Hence
$\Phi_r$ is an isotopy of germs, relative to $T$, from the identity to the
chart transition.  To make the punctured-neighborhood assertion uniform,
choose a compact germ domain over the entire named curve and then choose one
nonzero constant covector copy small enough that every $\Phi_r$ is defined
on it.  Each $\Phi_r$ fixes the zero section and is injective, so no point of
that copy can meet the zero section.  Compactness of the copy times
$[0,1]$ then gives a positive distance from the zero section throughout the
isotopy.  Thus the whole isotopy lies in one
$\nu(T)\smallsetminus T$ and proves that the two push-offs have the same
boundary class.  The first-jet symplectic calculation is replayed by the
checker listed in the supplement.  The potentially dangerous
momentum shift by a closed one-form could alter the pushed-off boundary
class, but it moves the zero section unless the form vanishes and is therefore
excluded here.  The Lagrangian-neighborhood statement in the first paragraph
of \cite[\S2.1]{ADK}, applied to each compact torus, supplies the required
zero-section Weinstein charts; the argument above supplies the precise
chart-independence consequence needed here.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Completion of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:audit-manifold}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:bundle-symplectic-form} makes the two disjoint audit tori
Lagrangian, and Theorem~\ref{thm:framing} identifies the two product
longitudes in Definition~\ref{def:Vaudit} with their Lagrangian-framing
classes.  The two unit-coefficient fillings are therefore Luttinger
surgeries in the sense of \cite[Definition~2.1]{ADK}; the symplectic form on
the filled manifold is the one asserted in \cite[Proposition~2.2]{ADK}.  That construction supplies a
symplectic form after each surgery which agrees with $\omega_K$ outside the
chosen torus neighborhood.  Performing the surgeries successively gives a
symplectic form on $V_{\mathrm{aud}}$.  The fiber $F$ chosen in the proof
above is disjoint from both surgery neighborhoods, so the resulting form
still restricts positively to $F$.
\end{proof}

\section{The audit double and the exotic $S^2\times S^2$}\label{sec:double}

This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:exotic}.  The boundary of
$V_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is the mapping torus of the boundary monodromy.  The
first subsection defines an involution of it from the clutching map
$\varphi_0$ alone and records the topology of the double.  The second shows
that the symplectic form of Lemma~\ref{lem:bundle-symplectic-form} descends
to the double, so that the four surgeries are Luttinger surgeries on a
closed symplectic surface bundle.  The third lists every external result
used, with the place where its hypotheses are discharged, and assembles the
proof.

\subsection{The intrinsic boundary involution}\label{sec:sigma-aud}

Throughout this section $\varphi_0$ and $\psi_0$ are the smooth
representatives of Lemma~\ref{lem:bundle-symplectic-form} on the smooth
surface $F$, and $R_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is the smooth bundle they clutch
(Corollary~\ref{cor:tori}).  Write $h\colon F\to F$ for the monodromy of
the boundary bundle $\partial R_{\mathrm{aud}}\to\partial\Sigma_{1,1}$,
the commutator of $\varphi_0$ and $\psi_0$ determined by the oriented
boundary word, a diffeomorphism, and
\[
 M_h=(F\times[0,1])/((z,1)\sim(h(z),0))
\]
for its mapping torus, a closed smooth 3-manifold with the base coordinate
$t\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, so that $\partial R_{\mathrm{aud}}=M_h$.
Since both surgeries take place in the interior,
$\partial V_{\mathrm{aud}}=M_h$ as well.

\begin{lemma}[the audit boundary involution]\label{lem:sigma-aud}
Define
\[
 \sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}\colon M_h\to M_h,\qquad
 \sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}[z,t]=[\varphi_0(z),\,1-t].
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is well defined, and is a
 fiber-preserving smooth involution of $M_h$ covering the reflection
 $t\mapsto 1-t$ of $\partial\Sigma_{1,1}$;
\item[(ii)] $\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$ reverses the orientation of
 $\partial R_{\mathrm{aud}}$;
\item[(iii)] $\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$ carries each of the circles
 $\{p\}\times S^1$ and $\{O\}\times S^1$, the boundaries of the two
 fixed-point sections, onto itself;
\item[(iv)] $\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$ fixes $[p,\tfrac12]$, $[O,\tfrac12]$,
 $[p,0]$, and $[O,0]$; in particular it is not free.
\end{enumerate}
No property of $\psi_0$ beyond its being a diffeomorphism fixing $p$ and $O$
is used, and no property of $\varphi_0$ beyond its being an
orientation-preserving smooth involution fixing $p$ and $O$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) The only identification in $M_h$ is $(z,1)\sim(h(z),0)$.  Under the
displayed formula, $(z,1)\mapsto(\varphi_0 z,0)$ while
$(h z,0)\mapsto(\varphi_0 h z,1)\sim(h\varphi_0 h z,0)$.  These agree for
every $z$ if and only if $\varphi_0=h\varphi_0 h$ as maps, that is,
using $\varphi_0^{-1}=\varphi_0$, if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma-seam}
 \varphi_0\,h\,\varphi_0^{-1}=h^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Now $h$ is a commutator of $\varphi_0$ and $\psi_0$.  For the ordering
$h=\varphi_0\psi_0\varphi_0^{-1}\psi_0^{-1}$,
\[
 \varphi_0\,h\,\varphi_0^{-1}
 =\varphi_0^{2}\,\psi_0\,\varphi_0^{-1}\psi_0^{-1}\varphi_0^{-1}
 =\psi_0\,\varphi_0\,\psi_0^{-1}\varphi_0
 =\bigl(\varphi_0^{-1}\psi_0\varphi_0\psi_0^{-1}\bigr)^{-1}
 =h^{-1},
\]
using only $\varphi_0^{2}=\mathrm{id}$; the other three orderings and
orientations of the commutator are checked in the same way (the machine
control \texttt{luttinger/sigma\_aud/sigma\_aud\_check.py} verifies all
four exactly on the based generators of $\pi_1(F,p)$, using the
certified monodromy actions).  So $\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is well defined.
It is smooth: away from the seam it is $\varphi_0\times(t\mapsto1-t)$;
in the seam chart $F\times(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)$ of $M_h$, in
which $(w,s)$ with $s>1$ denotes $(h(w),s-1)$, it reads
$(w,s)\mapsto(h^{-1}\varphi_0 w,\,2-s)$ for $s<1$ and
$(w,s)\mapsto(\varphi_0 h\,w,\,2-s)$ for $s>1$, and these are one smooth
map precisely because \eqref{eq:sigma-seam} holds as an identity of maps.
Being an involution it is a diffeomorphism.  (On the PL model it is PL
after subdividing $[0,1]$ symmetrically about $\tfrac12$; only the smooth
statement is used below.)  It preserves fibers and covers $t\mapsto1-t$ by
construction, and
$\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}^{2}[z,t]=[\varphi_0^{2}z,t]=[z,t]$.

(ii) Orient $M_h$ as $F\times[0,1]$, fiber first.  By
Definition~\ref{def:Vaudit}, $\varphi_0$ preserves the orientation of
$F$; the reflection reverses that of $[0,1]$; the product reverses.

(iii) $\varphi_0(p)=p$ and $\varphi_0(O)=O$.

(iv) A point $[z,t]$ is fixed only if $t=1-t$ in $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$,
so $t=\tfrac12$ or $t=0$.  Over $t=\tfrac12$ the fixed points are
$\{z:\varphi_0 z=z\}=\{p,O\}$.  Over the seam,
$\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}[z,0]=[\varphi_0 z,1]=[h\varphi_0 z,0]$, so the
fixed points are those of $h\varphi_0$; since $\varphi_0$ and $\psi_0$
both fix $p$ and $O$, so does $h$, and $p,O$ are among them.  (By
\eqref{eq:sigma-seam}, $(h\varphi_0)^2=h\,\varphi_0h\varphi_0=\mathrm{id}$,
so $h\varphi_0$ is itself an orientation-preserving involution of $F$;
its full fixed set is finite but is not needed.)
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
$\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is not free.  Freeness of a boundary involution is
needed only to form a quotient $W=V/\sigma$, as in the surgery scheme of
\cite{LidmanPiccirillo}; nothing in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:exotic}
uses it, and the quotient and everything downstream of it are outside the
scope of this article.  The supplement's historical transfer chain imports a
free involution through the attribution checklist; this article uses
$\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$ only.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[the audit double]\label{cor:audit-double}
Let $\hat\Sigma=\Sigma_{1,1}\cup_\rho\Sigma_{1,1}$ be the double of the
base along the reflection $\rho(t)=1-t$ of its boundary circle, a closed
oriented surface of genus two, and put
\[
 \hat R=R_{\mathrm{aud}}\cup_{\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}}R_{\mathrm{aud}},
 \qquad
 Z_{\mathrm{aud}}=V_{\mathrm{aud}}\cup_{\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}}V_{\mathrm{aud}},
\]
both copies carrying the orientation of $R_{\mathrm{aud}}$, glued along
the boundary diffeomorphism $\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:sigma-aud}, with the smooth structure on the union fixed by
the collar extension of Lemma~\ref{lem:double-form}(ii).  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\hat R$ is a closed oriented smooth genus-two surface bundle
 over $\hat\Sigma$, $\hat\Sigma$ has genus two, and $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$
 is a closed oriented smooth 4-manifold;
\item[(b)] $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is obtained from $\hat R$ by the two audit
 surgeries and their images under the swap of the two copies, performed on
 four pairwise disjoint tori in the interior of $\hat R$, all disjoint from
 a fiber $F$ over a base point away from $\alpha,\beta$ and their mirrors,
 and from the doubled section
 $\hat\Gamma=\Gamma\cup_{\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}}\Gamma$;
\item[(c)] $\hat\Gamma$ is a closed orientable surface of genus two with
 $\hat\Gamma\cdot\hat\Gamma=0$, $F\cdot F=0$, and $F\cdot\hat\Gamma=1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
(a) Gluing two copies of a smooth manifold with boundary along a
boundary diffeomorphism, with collars chosen as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:double-form}(ii), produces a closed smooth manifold, and
any two collar choices give diffeomorphic results; this is how $\hat R$
and $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ are smooth manifolds, and $V_{\mathrm{aud}}$
differs from $R_{\mathrm{aud}}$ only in its interior.  Gluing two copies
of a smooth bundle by a fiber-preserving diffeomorphism covering a
diffeomorphism of the base boundary produces a smooth bundle over the
glued base; here the base gluing is $\rho$, and
$\Sigma_{1,1}\cup_\rho\Sigma_{1,1}$ is the double of a once-punctured
torus, hence closed, orientable, of genus two.  Both
copies of $R_{\mathrm{aud}}$ induce the same orientation on the common
boundary, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:sigma-aud}(ii) the gluing map reverses
it, so $\hat R$ is oriented compatibly with both copies.  (Factorwise:
the base gluing $\rho$ reverses the boundary circle, so $\hat\Sigma$
restricts to the given orientation on each copy of $\Sigma_{1,1}$; the
fiber gluing $\varphi_0$ preserves the orientation of $F$; so neither
factor of the product orientation is reversed on the second copy.)

(b) $T_\alpha$ and $T_\beta$ lie over the spine loops $\alpha,\beta$,
which are interior to $\Sigma_{1,1}$, so both tori and their product
collars miss $\partial R_{\mathrm{aud}}$; the surgeries therefore commute
with the gluing, and their images under the swap are surgeries on the
mirror tori.  The four tori are disjoint: the two audit tori are disjoint
by construction and lie in one copy, their mirrors in the other, and the
two copies meet only along $\partial R_{\mathrm{aud}}$, which none of
them meets.  A fiber over a base point away from the four loops misses all
four tori.  The section $\Gamma$ through $p$ misses $T_\alpha$ and
$T_\beta$ because $p$ lies on neither $c$ nor $e$; the same holds in the
mirror copy; and by Lemma~\ref{lem:sigma-aud}(iii) the two halves glue
along $\partial\Gamma=\{p\}\times S^1$ to a closed surface $\hat\Gamma$.

(c) $\hat\Gamma$ is the double of the once-punctured torus $\Gamma$ along
its boundary circle, hence closed, orientable, of genus two.  For the
self-intersection, $\hat\Gamma\cdot\hat\Gamma$ is the Euler number of
the oriented normal bundle $\nu(\hat\Gamma)$.  Over each copy,
$\Gamma=\{p\}\times\Sigma_{1,1}$ is horizontal in the flat product
atlas of Lemma~\ref{lem:bundle-symplectic-form}, so $\nu(\Gamma)$ is the
flat oriented plane bundle with fiber $T_pF$ and holonomy
$d\varphi_0(p)$ along $\alpha$ and $d\psi_0(p)=I$ along $\beta$
($\psi_0$ is the identity near $p$).  Such a bundle is trivial, its base
being homotopy equivalent to a graph, and its holonomy around
$\partial\Gamma$ is the commutator $[d\varphi_0(p),d\psi_0(p)]=I$, so
the flat structure frames it along $\partial\Gamma$; that flat boundary
framing has degree zero relative to a global trivialization, because with
respect to an invariant inner product the connection form $A$ is a closed
real 1-form and the rotation is
$\frac1{2\pi}\oint_{\partial\Gamma}A=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\Gamma dA=0$.  Along
$\partial\Gamma=\{p\}\times S^1$ the gluing map $\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$
acts on $T_pF$ by the constant map $d\varphi_0(p)=-I$ ($\varphi_0$ is a
smooth involution with $p$ an isolated fixed point, so it is linearizable
at $p$ with derivative $-I$), which has degree zero in $SO(2)$.  Hence the
clutching map of $\nu(\hat\Gamma)$ between the two flat boundary framings
has degree zero and $\hat\Gamma\cdot\hat\Gamma=0$.  The certificate
\texttt{luttinger/pl\_self\_intersection.py} confirms this
combinatorially: it constructs $\hat\Gamma$ as a simplicial surface with
$f$-vector $(138,420,280)$, on whose $24$-cycle link of $p$ in the fiber
triangulation $\varphi_0$ acts by the half-turn (shift $12$), i.e.\ by
$-I$; it builds the triangulated normal disk bundle of $\hat\Gamma$ for
each of the four constant boundary clutching rotations, including this one
(its ``constant clutch shift $2$''), and in each case exhibits a disjoint
simplicial push-off together with the radial PL cylinder joining it to the
zero section, so the signed intersection chain is empty.  A fiber has
trivial normal bundle, so
$F\cdot F=0$, and a fiber meets a section transversally in one point, so
$F\cdot\hat\Gamma=1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The symplectic form on the audit double}\label{sec:symplectic-double}

Keep the notation of Lemma~\ref{lem:sigma-aud} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:audit-double}: $\hat\Sigma=\Sigma_{1,1}\cup_\rho\Sigma_{1,1}$,
$\hat R=R_{\mathrm{aud}}\cup_{\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}}R_{\mathrm{aud}}$,
$Z_{\mathrm{aud}}=V_{\mathrm{aud}}\cup_{\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}}V_{\mathrm{aud}}$,
with $\hat\pi\colon\hat R\to\hat\Sigma$ the projection and $F$, $\hat\Gamma$
the fiber and doubled section of that corollary.

\begin{lemma}[symplectic form on the audit double]\label{lem:double-form}
Let $\Omega$ be the positive area form on $F$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:bundle-symplectic-form}, preserved by $\varphi_0$ and
$\psi_0$.  Choose the positive area form $\eta$ on $\Sigma_{1,1}$ so that on
a collar $\partial\Sigma_{1,1}\times(-\varepsilon,0]$ with boundary parameter
$t\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ and outward-increasing collar coordinate $s$ it
equals $ds\wedge dt$, and so that the collar is disjoint from the annular
collars of $\alpha,\beta$ used in that lemma.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\eta$ on each copy glues to a smooth positive area form
 $\hat\eta$ on $\hat\Sigma$;
\item[(ii)] the vertical form $\Omega_{\mathrm{vert}}$ on each copy glues to
 a smooth closed vertical form $\hat\Omega_{\mathrm{vert}}$ on $\hat R$;
\item[(iii)] for every $K>0$,
 $\hat\omega_K=\hat\Omega_{\mathrm{vert}}+K\hat\pi^*\hat\eta$ is a
 symplectic form on $\hat R$ restricting to $\omega_K$ on each copy;
\item[(iv)] $\hat\omega_K$ is positive on $F$ and on $\hat\Gamma$;
\item[(v)] the four surgery tori of Corollary~\ref{cor:audit-double}(b) are
 Lagrangian for $\hat\omega_K$, and each has a protected neighborhood with
 the split coordinates of Lemma~\ref{lem:bundle-symplectic-form}, disjoint
 from the seam collar $\hat\pi^{-1}(\partial\Sigma_{1,1}\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) On the seam collar of $\hat\Sigma$ use the chart
$(t,s)\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$ in which
$s\le0$ is the first copy's collar and, for $s\ge0$, the point $(t,s)$ is the
second copy's collar point with its own coordinates $t'=\rho(t)=1-t$ and
$s'=-s$: the second copy's outward direction is toward the seam, so its
outward-increasing coordinate is $-s$.  In its own coordinates the second
copy's form is $ds'\wedge dt'=d(-s)\wedge d(1-t)=ds\wedge dt$.  So the two
copies of $\eta$ are the single form $ds\wedge dt$ on the whole chart, and
$\hat\eta$ is smooth.  It is positive on both copies since each is oriented
as $\Sigma_{1,1}$ and $\hat\Sigma$ restricts to those orientations
(Corollary~\ref{cor:audit-double}(a)).

(ii) Over the seam collar the flat product atlas of
Lemma~\ref{lem:bundle-symplectic-form} presents the bundle as
$M_h\times(-\varepsilon,0]$ with $\Omega_{\mathrm{vert}}$ equal to $\Omega$
on every fiber; this is well defined because the seam map $h$ of $M_h$ is a
product of the clutching maps and so preserves $\Omega$.  Extend the gluing map over the two collars as
$(z,t,s)\mapsto(\varphi_0 z,1-t,-s)$; this fixes the smooth structure of
$\hat R$ near the seam (any two collar choices give diffeomorphic doubles),
and in the flat atlas it is, on every fiber, the single map $\varphi_0$,
which is locally constant in the base and preserves $\Omega$.  Hence the transition maps of the flat atlas of $\hat R$
obtained by adjoining $\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$ all lie in the group generated
by $\varphi_0$ and $\psi_0$, all preserve $\Omega$, and the fiberwise form
$\Omega$ descends to a globally defined vertical form
$\hat\Omega_{\mathrm{vert}}$ on $\hat R$.  It is closed because in each flat
product chart it is the pullback of the closed form $\Omega$ from the fiber
factor.

(iii) $d\hat\omega_K=0$ by (i) and (ii), and with the fiber and base
orientations fixed by $\Omega$ and $\hat\eta$,
$\hat\omega_K^2=2K\,\hat\Omega_{\mathrm{vert}}\wedge\hat\pi^*\hat\eta>0$,
exactly as in Lemma~\ref{lem:bundle-symplectic-form}.  On each copy the form
is $\Omega_{\mathrm{vert}}+K\pi^*\eta=\omega_K$ by construction.

(iv) $F$ is a fiber, on which $\hat\omega_K$ restricts to $\Omega>0$.
$\hat\Gamma$ is the doubled section through the common fixed point $p$; in
every flat product chart it is $\{p\}\times U$, so
$\hat\Omega_{\mathrm{vert}}$ restricts to zero on it and
$\hat\omega_K|_{\hat\Gamma}=K\hat\eta>0$.

(v) Each of the four tori lies in the interior of one copy, over the
annular collar of $\alpha$ or $\beta$ in that copy, which is disjoint from
the seam collar by the choice of $\eta$.  On that copy $\hat\omega_K=\omega_K$,
so Lemma~\ref{lem:bundle-symplectic-form} applies verbatim: the torus has
one fiber tangent and one base tangent, both summands vanish on it, and the
protected split coordinates are the ones displayed there.  The mirror tori
are the images of $T_\alpha,T_\beta$ under the swap of the two copies, which
is an isomorphism of the second copy with $R_{\mathrm{aud}}$ carrying
$\hat\omega_K|_{\text{copy }2}$ to $\omega_K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[$Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is symplectic]\label{cor:Z-symplectic}
$Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ carries a symplectic form $\hat\omega$ that agrees with
$\hat\omega_K$ outside the four surgery neighborhoods, restricts positively
to $F$ and to $\hat\Gamma$, and is obtained from $(\hat R,\hat\omega_K)$ by
four Luttinger surgeries in the sense of \cite[Definition~2.1]{ADK}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:double-form}(v) and Theorem~\ref{thm:framing} applied in
each copy, the four tori are Lagrangian and the four unit-coefficient filling
slopes are their Lagrangian-framing classes; the surgeries of
Corollary~\ref{cor:audit-double}(b) are therefore Luttinger surgeries, and
\cite[Proposition~2.2]{ADK} supplies, one surgery at a time, a symplectic
form agreeing with the previous one outside the surgered neighborhood.  The
four neighborhoods are disjoint from one another, from $F$, and from
$\hat\Gamma$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:audit-double}(b)), so the final form still
restricts positively to $F$ and $\hat\Gamma$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:double-form}(iv).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hypotheses of the external results, discharged}
\label{sec:external-hypotheses}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:exotic} uses six results from the
literature.  Each is stated with its hypotheses, and each hypothesis is
discharged by a statement about the audit object proved in this article.
No comparison with any other author's construction enters.

\begin{description}
\item[Van Kampen.] $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}=V_{\mathrm{aud}}\cup_{\partial}V_{\mathrm{aud}}$
 with connected, bicollared common boundary $\partial V_{\mathrm{aud}}=M_h$
 and $\pione(V_{\mathrm{aud}})=1$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:audit-manifold});
 hence $\pione(Z_{\mathrm{aud}})=1$.
\item[Freedman \cite{Freedman}.] Closed simply connected topological
 4-manifolds are classified by intersection form and Kirby--Siebenmann
 invariant, the latter determined by $\sigma/8$ when the form is even.
 Hypotheses discharged in Proposition~\ref{prop:Z-topology}: simple
 connectivity, $b_2=2$, form $H$ on the basis $(F,\hat\Gamma)$,
 smoothness (so Kirby--Siebenmann vanishes).
\item[Thurston \cite{Thurston}.] A closed surface bundle over a surface with
 homologically essential fiber is symplectic with symplectic fibers.  We do
 not invoke it: Lemma~\ref{lem:double-form} constructs the form directly
 (the fiber is essential anyway, since $F\cdot\hat\Gamma=1$).
\item[Auroux--Donaldson--Katzarkov \cite{ADK} (Luttinger \cite{Luttinger}).]
 Luttinger surgery on a Lagrangian torus along its Lagrangian framing yields
 a symplectic manifold with a form agreeing with the old one away from the
 torus.  Hypotheses discharged in Lemma~\ref{lem:double-form}(v) and
 Theorem~\ref{thm:framing}: Lagrangian tori, Lagrangian-framed slopes.
\item[{Ho--Li \cite[Theorem~1.1]{HoLi}.}] Luttinger surgery preserves the
 symplectic Kodaira dimension.  The surgery there is the one of
 \cite[Section~2.1]{HoLi}, which follows \cite{ADK}, performed on a
 Lagrangian torus of a closed symplectic 4-manifold.  Hypotheses discharged
 in Corollary~\ref{cor:Z-symplectic}: each of the four surgeries is a
 Luttinger surgery in the sense of \cite[Definition~2.1]{ADK} on a
 Lagrangian torus of the closed symplectic manifold produced by the
 previous one.
\item[{Liu \cite{Liu}, as stated in \cite[Theorem~2.4(2)]{LiClay}.}] A
 minimal closed symplectic 4-manifold has Kodaira dimension $-\infty$ only
 if it is rational or ruled, where rational and ruled are defined by the
 underlying smooth manifold ($S^2\times S^2$, $\CP^2\#k\,\overline{\CP}{}^2$,
 or an $S^2$-bundle over a surface blown up $k\ge0$ times).  Hypothesis:
 minimality, discharged for $(\hat R,\hat\omega_K)$ in Step~1 of the proof
 below by asphericity.
\item[{Li \cite{Li}, \cite[p.~251]{LiClay}.}] The Kodaira dimension of a
 closed symplectic 4-manifold depends only on the oriented diffeomorphism
 type: two symplectic forms on one closed oriented 4-manifold, both
 compatible with its orientation, have the same $\kappa$.  Hypothesis: a
 common orientation, arranged in Step~3 of the proof below.
\end{description}

\begin{proposition}[topology of $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$]\label{prop:Z-topology}
$Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is closed, simply connected, and spin, with
$b_2(Z_{\mathrm{aud}})=2$, signature $0$, and intersection form $H$ on the
basis $(F,\hat\Gamma)$.  Consequently $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is homeomorphic to
$S^2\times S^2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Closed: $\partial V_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is glued to itself.  Simply connected
by van Kampen as above.  $\chi(Z_{\mathrm{aud}})=2\chi(V_{\mathrm{aud}})-\chi(M_h)=4-0=4$
(the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:audit-manifold} gives
$\chi(V_{\mathrm{aud}})=2$; a mapping torus has Euler characteristic $0$),
so $b_2=2$ with $H_2$ torsion-free.  By
Corollary~\ref{cor:audit-double}(c), $F\cdot F=0$, $\hat\Gamma\cdot\hat\Gamma=0$,
$F\cdot\hat\Gamma=1$: the Gram matrix of $(F,\hat\Gamma)$ is $H$, of
determinant $-1$, hence unimodular; a unimodular rank-two sublattice of the
unimodular rank-two lattice $H_2(Z_{\mathrm{aud}})$ has index one, so
$(F,\hat\Gamma)$ is a basis and the form is $H$.  $H$ is even, so
$w_2=0$ by Wu's formula and $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is spin; its signature is
$0$.  $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is smooth, so its Kirby--Siebenmann invariant
vanishes, and Freedman's classification gives
$Z_{\mathrm{aud}}\cong S^2\times S^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:exotic}]
Symplectic and homeomorphic to $S^2\times S^2$ by
Corollary~\ref{cor:Z-symplectic} and Proposition~\ref{prop:Z-topology}.
For non-diffeomorphism we use the symplectic Kodaira dimension $\kappa$ of
\cite{Li}: for a minimal closed symplectic 4-manifold $(M,\omega)$ with
canonical class $K_\omega$, $\kappa(M,\omega)=-\infty$ if and only if
$K_\omega\cdot[\omega]<0$ or $K_\omega^2<0$
\cite[Definition~2.2]{LiClay}.  Throughout, a manifold is oriented and its
symplectic forms are compatible with that orientation.

\emph{Step 1: $\kappa(\hat R,\hat\omega_K)\ne-\infty$.}  $\hat R$ is a
surface bundle with aspherical fiber and base, hence aspherical.  A
symplectic 4-manifold with $\pi_2=0$ is minimal (an exceptional sphere
would be null-homotopic, hence null-homologous, of square $0$, not $-1$)
and is neither rational nor ruled: each of those contains an embedded
sphere that is homologically nonzero (a factor sphere, a line, an
exceptional sphere, or a fiber of an $S^2$-bundle, which meets a section
once), so has $\pi_2\ne0$.  By Liu's theorem
\cite{Liu}, \cite[Theorem~2.4(2)]{LiClay}, a minimal symplectic 4-manifold
with $\kappa=-\infty$ is rational or ruled.  Hence
$\kappa(\hat R,\hat\omega_K)\ne-\infty$.

\emph{Step 2: $\kappa(Z_{\mathrm{aud}},\hat\omega)\ne-\infty$.}  By
Corollary~\ref{cor:Z-symplectic}, $(Z_{\mathrm{aud}},\hat\omega)$ is
obtained from $(\hat R,\hat\omega_K)$ by four successive Luttinger
surgeries, each on a Lagrangian torus of the closed symplectic 4-manifold
produced by the previous one.  Each preserves $\kappa$
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{HoLi}.

\emph{Step 3: a pulled-back product form.}  Suppose
$\psi\colon Z_{\mathrm{aud}}\to S^2\times S^2$ were a diffeomorphism.
Orient $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ by $\hat\omega$ and $S^2\times S^2$ as a product
of oriented spheres.  If $\psi$ reverses orientation, replace it by
$(r\times\mathrm{id})\circ\psi$ with $r$ a reflection of $S^2$; so
$\psi$ may be taken orientation-preserving.  Let
$\omega_{a,b}=a\,\mathrm{pr}_1^*\sigma+b\,\mathrm{pr}_2^*\sigma$
($a,b>0$, $\sigma$ an area form of total area $1$ on $S^2$), a symplectic
form on $S^2\times S^2$ compatible with its orientation.  Then
$\psi^*\omega_{a,b}$ is a symplectic form on $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ compatible
with the $\hat\omega$-orientation, and
$\kappa(Z_{\mathrm{aud}},\psi^*\omega_{a,b})=\kappa(S^2\times S^2,\omega_{a,b})$
since $\psi$ is a symplectomorphism.  The latter is $-\infty$ by the
definition: the intersection form of $S^2\times S^2$ is even, so no class
has square $-1$ and the manifold is minimal; and the canonical class of the
product form evaluates to $-\chi(S^2)=-2$ on each factor sphere, so
$K\cdot[\omega_{a,b}]=-2a-2b<0$.

\emph{Step 4.}  $\hat\omega$ and $\psi^*\omega_{a,b}$ are symplectic forms
on $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ compatible with one orientation, so
$\kappa(Z_{\mathrm{aud}},\hat\omega)=\kappa(Z_{\mathrm{aud}},\psi^*\omega_{a,b})$
by \cite{Li}, \cite[p.~251]{LiClay}: the Kodaira dimension depends only on
the oriented diffeomorphism type.  Steps~2 and~3 make the two sides
$\ne-\infty$ and $=-\infty$.  Hence no such $\psi$ exists, and
$Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is not diffeomorphic to $S^2\times S^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what the theorem does and does not say]
Theorem~\ref{thm:exotic} is a statement about the object of
Definition~\ref{def:Vaudit} and its double along $\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$.
It does not assert that $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is the manifold proposed by
Wuebben \cite{Wuebben}; that attribution question is the subject of
Section~\ref{sec:relation}, and no statement there enters this proof.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The reported contrary computation and live failure locations}
\label{sec:contrary}

Wuebben's public repository reports that Lidman and Piccirillo obtained a
fundamental-group calculation disagreeing with his, currently summarized as
$\pione(V)\ne1$.  Their published paper \cite{LidmanPiccirillo} proves
$H_1(V)=0$ and normal generation by $\pione(F)$ but does not publish this
contrary presentation.  No relation sheet, generator dictionary, or
nontriviality witness for the reported calculation is available in the
materials examined here.

The finite Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is unaffected because its objects are
explicit strings, and Theorem~\ref{thm:exotic} is a statement about the
explicit object $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$.  A contrary calculation about a
different member says nothing about either; one about the same member would
locate an error in one of the two computations.  If the contrary calculation
concerns the same geometric member, the live explanations are deliberately left neutral: a
source-to-audit clause may fail; a displayed geometric identification or
finite checker may be defective; the contrary calculation may be defective;
or the computations may concern different members or conventions.  A
relation-by-relation reconciliation requires the missing presentation,
dictionary, and witness.  Until that material exists, this article makes no
claim about Wuebben's $V$.

\section{Conclusion}

Four manifest-bound presentations are trivial under a complete certificate
specification.  A separate geometric reconstruction identifies one of them
with the explicitly defined $V_{\mathrm{aud}}$, yielding the intrinsic
topological statements of Theorem~\ref{thm:audit-manifold}; the framing
theorem supplies its symplectic structure.  Doubling along the intrinsic
boundary involution $\sigma_{\mathrm{aud}}$ and descending that form gives
Theorem~\ref{thm:exotic}: $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$ is a closed symplectic
four-manifold homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to $S^2\times S^2$.  The
separation between finite derivation, geometric conversion, symplectic
framing, and doubling is the article's contribution; the final
classification and non-diffeomorphism deductions use named external
theorems whose hypotheses are discharged in the text.

Whether Wuebben's text and diagrams specify the same marked smooth surgery
data, and whether his double coincides with $Z_{\mathrm{aud}}$, remain
open.  The supplement records that attribution question and the historical
transfer chain without promoting either into a theorem of this article.
